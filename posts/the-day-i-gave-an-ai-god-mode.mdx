It was night-time, my midsems were over, and I was playing a shooting game with my friends. I just wanted to relax after having some heavy exams. I used to be a great player in that game, but that was a very long time ago. And I was not performing well in the game. My friend taunted me, “Tu toh accha player tha na.”

One thought ran into my mind: why are we making everything so performative? Why can't we just play for fun and relax? I know he was just joking, but still, it happens in a lot of different places too. Take music, for example. If someone asks you to play music on the speaker, suddenly you are under a lot of peer pressure. What music should I play? What if they don't like it? What if they think, “Oh, so this is the type of music he listens to”? The same thing happens with food. Sometimes we order something we don't even want to eat, just because the other food is considered cool.

## Root Cause

I think one of the main root causes of this issue is social media. On social media, people mostly show the highlighted parts of their lives or the things they have carefully performed. They don't show the failures before cracking an exam, getting an internship, or landing a job. 

They don't tell you about the self-doubt they had at night, the kind that doesn't let them sleep. They don't show you the worst parts, because then other people might judge them. And it's a loop. You are getting judged, and you are judging people too. People will judge someone and say, “He is so weak,” “She is a failure,” “You know, she did this, he did that,” and so on. And this is our biggest fear: what will others say?

And the funny part is, we are the others. We are the ones cherishing perfection. It is our collective effort. Friends, society, the system-everyone is conveying the same message: You are not allowed to fail

## What I think about this

I am not saying that you should stop judging people or that you should appreciate and accept everything. I am just saying that sometimes it is okay to let people be who they are. Let them pronounce something wrong. Let them fail. Let them make mistakes. Because we are not machines. We are humans. Humans will make mistakes. We are imperfect. No one can be a perfect person all the time.

Also, after that thought, I played really well. I don't know why.

This is my first article, and I am intentionally keeping it short, because I am not writing some self-help book here. I am just writing what I felt.
